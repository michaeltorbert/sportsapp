# Watchlist ordering decisions

This is the cumulative product contract and conflict log. The numeric implementation is documented in [watchlist-priority.md](watchlist-priority.md). Regression coverage lives in [watch-priority.test.mjs](../tests/watch-priority.test.mjs). Repository [AGENTS.md](../AGENTS.md) requires reading and updating this log for every ordering change.

## How to use this record

1. Append the next stable ORD ID for a new instruction. Record the actual wording or faithful paraphrase and date; label uncertain interpretations.
2. Compare against every active rule below. Preserve existing rules unless an explicit new instruction supersedes one. Describe every exception and conflict; ambiguous conflicts need user direction before implementation.
3. Add a test for the requested comparison and run all old and new ordering tests plus the full suite. A failed older test is evidence of a conflict to resolve, not permission to erase it.
4. Add a dated change entry naming the IDs affected, decisions and validation. Commit the log, tests, code and instructions together. Earlier versions and exact code diffs remain in Git history.

## Conflict and supersession history

### 2026-09-19 — ORD-012 Power Four relevance, rank disruption and slate alert priority

- Source: issues #82 and #74, authorized final planning candidate; active. Numeric calibration and the global gate are coordinator interpretations accepted in that plan, not verbatim user numbers.
- Taxonomy: [NCAA, How the College Football Playoff works](https://www.ncaa.com/news/football/article/2026-02-03/how-college-football-playoff-works-schedule-selections-rankings-byes-and-more), updated 2026-02-03 and verified 2026-09-19, identifies Power Four (ACC, Big 12, Big Ten, SEC) and Group of Six (American, CUSA, MAC, Mountain West, Pac-12, Sun Belt). ESPN group IDs are respectively 1/4/5/8 and 151/12/15/17/9/37. Independents, FCS, unknown conference and unknown ranking evidence are not automatically two-unranked Group of Six.
- Display: preserve state groups, Duke/VT pins, ACC 20/22 and the graded comparator. Replace SEC-only 4 with non-ACC Power Four 4 once per game. Close late Group-of-Six drama can still outrank comfortable Power-Four wins. No binary conference tier or category change.
- #74: separate `disruption` from favorite-based `upset`. Known worse-ranked or unranked leaders can add rank interest even when the line favored them; labels stay truthful. Weight 35 is the smallest whole-point weight satisfying both assumed narrow-line Iowa State–Iowa versus Ohio State–Texas fixtures with the existing stage/margin and 75% tie / 50% late-narrow-lead factors. No display spread cutoff. The ordinary fixture now defaults explicitly to American rather than silently Big 12.
- Precise supersession: ORD-009's favorite-only restriction on ordering interest is superseded only for the separate rank-disruption component. ORD-012 also removes the prior additional ranked-trailing interest attached to a betting favorite when its opponent is better-ranked or has unknown rank: favorite-based significance remains, but those states do not establish rank disruption. This is the accepted separation of line and rank evidence, not a change to truthful labels or alert conditions/IDs; a ranked betting underdog still has `upset === 0`. The focused worse-ranked-favorite and unknown-rank-opponent regressions in `tests/watch-priority.test.mjs` protect this boundary. ORD-001–008, ORD-010 and ORD-011 remain active with their recorded exceptions. ORD-008 remains pending.
- Alerts: only the delivery gate for two-known-unranked Group-of-Six `one-score-fourth` events changes. A current stronger live candidate must also have a fresh event. The immutable global decision precedes recipient claims, including when a recipient disabled/already received the stronger trigger. A skipped event has no claim and only its original three-minute window, with no refresh or replay after expiry. Duke remains hard-off, and raw conditions, transitions, IDs, baselines, activation and authoritative atomic claim SQL are preserved.
- Coverage: `ORD-012 exact conference taxonomy and bounded Power Four relevance preserve late drama`, `ORD-012 issue 74 rank disruption is line-independent and labels remain truthful`, retained contrary-line `upset === 0` coverage in `ranked upset watch precedes Texas A&M winning in the reported third-quarter state`, and all six `ORD-012` cases in `tests/alert-competition.test.mjs`. Ordering covers reversed pairs and all three-game permutations. Preferences retain all 16 combinations and concurrency/sibling/activation regressions; source-copy and browser disclosure tests cover the global policy.

### 2026-09-19 — ORD-011 independent category toggles with an All reset

- Source: explicit user request in issue #73, active. The toggle/reset behavior is quoted; OR combination, the empty-selection fallback to All, keeping four manual toggles selected, and the All presentation are the accepted issue recommendation. The date-scope decision is recorded here before implementation, as the issue required.
- Period decision (assistant interpretation, reviewed by Claude Fable 5.1 medium on 2026-09-19 and chosen by the user): categories and period are independent. A Day | Week control in the date bar replaces the implicit “ACC and Top 25 are weekly” rule. Day shows the selected Eastern date; Week shows the current Thursday–Monday football week for every selected category. Nothing switches the period implicitly, so the date label always describes every displayed game and count. Legacy links keep their meaning: `tab=acc` and `tab=top25` open Week with that category, `tab=close` and `tab=upset` open Day with that category, `tab=watch` or an unknown value opens Day with All. New links and app-update restoration carry `cats=` and `period=` explicitly. Home returns to Day and All. A week containing a manually selected date is a deferred enhancement, not the meaning of Week.
- Data decision: under Week every category filters the existing full-FBS weekly feed (ESPN `groups=80`, `limit=200`). The separate ACC-only weekly request was retired because ACC games are a subset of that board; the CDN fallback already derived the ACC board the same way. Two requests per refresh replace three. A response that fills the requested limit now adds a visible “list may be incomplete” warning. Retained final categories carry over from the pre-1.9 weekly storage key.
- Counts: every count describes the displayed period under the current Hide finals and Duke visibility rules. All is the deduplicated union of the four categories plus the All-only focused-game exception; category toggles never inherit that exception. Unloaded counts remain unknown rather than zero.
- Ordering: ORD-001 through ORD-010 are unchanged. Combined selections show each game once in the shared order, and matching several categories adds no priority (regression named above). Alert eligibility, trigger IDs and phone notifications are unaffected; the existing `expanded list eligibility does not redefine ranked push triggers or their dedupe IDs` guard remains.
- Supersession: ORD-011 supersedes the imported single-tab behavior, including the baseline’s mixed daily/weekly counts across tabs. The `Watchlist` tab label becomes `All`; the All view keeps its “Your watchlist” heading. No earlier ordering comparison is weakened; string-filter regressions were translated to selections and extended with combined selections.
- Validation: Claude ran `npm test` (298 passed, 0 failed), lint, typecheck and the complete Playwright suite on Chromium and WebKit mobile emulation (104 passed each). This is the implementer’s own run, not an independent review verdict.
